\section*{Summary}
A discrepancy was reported between the analytic 1D half-space transient EM response and a SimPEG forward
model (\texttt{comparision.ipynb}). We traced it to the \emph{time-stepping configuration} of the 3D
forward model --- not a code defect --- and fixed it with a one-line change. Two conclusions matter:

\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{0.2em}
  \item \textbf{It is not a bug in SimPEG.} Both solvers compute the half-space response correctly. The 1D
  layered solution reproduces the closed-form Ward \& Hohmann response to \SI{7e-4}{\percent} (circular
  loop), and the 3D source/assembly converges to the exact field as the mesh refines
  (Section~\ref{sec:notbug}). The original model was simply under-resolved \emph{in time}.
  \item \textbf{The time-stepping must be designed conservatively.} The original ramp doubled the step size
  each level ($\times2$, 55 steps), which is too aggressive for backward-Euler and biases $\dbdt$ high by
  $\sim$20\,\%. A gentler $\times1.4$ ramp (156 steps) removes it: against the same-geometry SimPEG 1D
  \emph{square} solution the corrected response is within $\sim$3\,\% at every channel
  (Figure~\ref{fig:hero}), and against the closed-form \emph{circular} analytic it is within $\sim$2\,\% on
  average (Figure~\ref{fig:hero_circle}). Refining the \emph{mesh} instead barely helps, and pushing the
  steps \emph{arbitrarily} fine over-corrects --- so the rule is conservative \emph{and} well-resolved, not
  finest-possible (Section~\ref{sec:onepct}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Naming.} ``SimPEG'' labels only curves we \emph{compute by running SimPEG} (the 1D layered
solution and the 3D octree re-runs). The \textbf{committed solution} is loaded from
\texttt{half\_tem\_faquarson.csv} --- itself produced by a SimPEG 3D run
(\texttt{forward\_farquarson.py}), which our square re-runs reproduce to \SI{0.08}{\percent}. (In
\texttt{comparision.ipynb} the curve labelled \texttt{analytic-half}, \texttt{dpred}, is in fact the SimPEG
1D layered result --- the accurate one.)

\section{It is not a bug: the SimPEG solvers are validated}
\label{sec:notbug}
Three independent checks establish that both the 1D and 3D solvers compute the correct half-space response,
so the discrepancy cannot be a formulation or assembly error.

\paragraph{(i) The 1D layered solution is exact.} The vertical $\dbdt$ at the centre of a loop on a
homogeneous half-space after a step-off (Ward \& Hohmann, 1988) is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial b_z}{\partial t} = -\frac{1}{\sigma a^3}
  \left[\,3\,\mathrm{erf}(\theta a) - \frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,\theta a\,(3 + 2\theta^2 a^2)\,e^{-\theta^2 a^2}\right],
  \qquad \theta = \sqrt{\frac{\mu_0 \sigma}{4t}},
\end{equation}
with $\sigma=\SI{0.1}{S/m}$ and equal-area radius $a=\sqrt{A/\pi}=\SI{56.42}{m}$ for the
$\SI{100}{m}\times\SI{100}{m}$ loop. Running \texttt{Simulation1DLayered} with a \emph{circular} loop of
radius $a$ reproduces this closed form to a maximum error of \SI{7e-4}{\percent} (Figure~\ref{fig:wh1d}).
The 1D code is therefore the trustworthy reference; with the actual \emph{square} \texttt{LineCurrent} it
differs by \SI{1.82}{\percent} at the earliest time --- real geometry, addressed in
Section~\ref{sec:geom}.

\begin{figure}[H]\centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_wardhohmann_1d.png}
  \caption{\textbf{The 1D layered solution reproduces the closed form.} SimPEG's 1D circular-loop response
  (markers) on the Ward \& Hohmann analytic (line); right, their difference, below \SI{1e-3}{\percent} at
  every channel.}
  \label{fig:wh1d}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{(ii) The 3D source and assembly are correct.} For a step-off the initial condition is the
magnetostatic loop field; at the centre of a circular loop $B_z = \mu_0 I/(2a)$ exactly. The
\emph{discretized} initial $B_z$ at the receiver (no time-stepping involved) converges monotonically to
this closed form as the mesh refines --- $+2.20\,\%$ at \SI{11}{k} cells down to $+0.27\,\%$ at
\SI{190}{k} (Figure~\ref{fig:init}). This is textbook spatial convergence, confirming the
\texttt{CircularLoop} source (which uses the finite-loop \texttt{CircularLoopWholeSpace} vector potential,
not a point-dipole approximation) and the field assembly are implemented correctly.

\begin{figure}[H]\centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.72\textwidth]{fig_initial_field_convergence.png}
  \caption{\textbf{The 3D circular-loop initial field converges to the exact $\mu_0 I/(2a)$} as the octree
  refines --- spatial discretization, not a code bug.}
  \label{fig:init}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{(iii) With proper settings, the 3D solution matches the references.} Once the time-stepping is
adequate, the 3D octree \emph{square}-loop response lands on the same-geometry 1D square solution
(Figure~\ref{fig:hero}) and on the closed-form circular analytic (Figure~\ref{fig:hero_circle};
Section~\ref{sec:fix}).
\textbf{There is no defect in either the 1D or the 3D solver}; the original discrepancy was a time
discretization that was too coarse.

\section{The cause and the fix: conservative time-stepping}
\label{sec:fix}
\texttt{forward\_farquarson.py} builds a tree mesh, paints conductivity by volume-averaging from a
background tensor mesh, drives a \texttt{LineCurrent} source, and solves with
\texttt{Simulation3DElectricField}. Its original time discretization doubles the step size each level ---
55 steps, $\times2$ --- which is too aggressive for the backward-Euler scheme to resolve the diffusing
response, biasing $\dbdt$ high. The single line setting \texttt{time\_steps} was replaced with a gentler
ramp:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{minipage}{0.93\textwidth}
\texttt{\textcolor{red}{- }generate\_time\_steps(n\_constant\_steps=\textbf{11}, increase\_rate=\textbf{2},\ \ \ start\_time\_step=\textbf{1e-6}, n\_per\_step=\textbf{5})}\\
\texttt{\textcolor{blue}{+ }generate\_time\_steps(n\_constant\_steps=\textbf{26}, increase\_rate=\textbf{1.4}, start\_time\_step=\textbf{3e-7}, n\_per\_step=\textbf{6})}
\end{minipage}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Time-steps, not mesh.} To isolate the temporal error from any geometry, we run the 3D octree
with a \emph{circular} loop and compare against the \emph{exact} analytic (Table~\ref{tab:conv},
Figure~\ref{fig:conv}). Refining the time-steps on the original mesh drives the error down monotonically
($26\,\% \to 6\,\%$); refining the \emph{mesh} ($3.4\times$ cells) on the original time-steps barely moves
it ($26\,\% \to 24\,\%$). The lever is unambiguously the time discretization.

\noindent Against the circular analytic the square benchmark lands even closer --- mean $0.996$
(Figure~\ref{fig:hero_circle}) --- because the square-loop geometry sits $\sim$1.8\,\% \emph{below} the
equal-area circle and partly cancels the spatial floor (Sections~\ref{sec:geom}--\ref{sec:onepct}).

\section{The residual $\sim$1.8\,\% is real square-vs-circle geometry, not error}
\label{sec:geom}
The square loop \emph{is} modelled accurately; the residual early-time gap is geometry. Two facts establish
this (Figures~\ref{fig:diag}--\ref{fig:geom}):

\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.1em}
  \item \textbf{The wire integration is converged.} Refining the Gauss--Legendre quadrature along each
  segment (\texttt{n\_points\_per\_path} $=3\to10\to100$) changes the 1D square-loop response by
  $<0.07\,\%$ (already $<10^{-4}\,\%$ by 10 points). The \SI{1.82}{\percent} is the \emph{true} square-loop
  response, not an artifact; and a circular loop reproduces the analytic to \SI{7e-4}{\percent}.
  \item \textbf{There is no elementary closed form for a square loop.} The ``analytic'' curve is for a
  \emph{circle}. A square and its equal-area circle coincide only in the late-time point-dipole limit; at
  early time the central $\dbdt$ depends on the actual distance to the wire (sides at \SI{50}{m}, corners
  at \SI{70.7}{m}, versus a circle at \SI{56.4}{m} everywhere).
\end{itemize}

\noindent The square matches its equal-area circle to $0.03\,\%$ late but sits $\sim$1.8\,\% below it
early, bracketed by the inscribed and circumscribed circles throughout. Using the equal-area circle as the
\emph{early-time} reference manufactures the ``error''; the correct apples-to-apples comparison ---
1D layered square vs 3D octree square --- removes it (the $+0.2\,\%$ mean in Section~\ref{sec:onepct}).

\section{Reaching $<1\,\%$: the spatial floor, and why finer is not better}
\label{sec:onepct}
Scored against the correct same-geometry reference (the 1D layered \emph{square} loop), the fix sits at
mean $+0.2\,\%$ (max $3.0\,\%$). Pushing the steps \emph{finer} does not improve this --- it overshoots:

\begin{table}[H]\centering\small
\caption{Refining time-steps past the fix (coarse mesh), versus the 1D layered \emph{square} loop.}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Time steps & \# steps & Mean error & Max error \\
\midrule
$\times1.4$, start \SI{3e-7}{s}  (the fix) & 156 & $+0.2\,\%$ & $3.0\,\%$ \\
$\times1.3$, start \SI{1e-7}{s}            & 198 & $-2.4\,\%$ & $5.8\,\%$ \\
$\times1.25$, start \SI{7e-8}{s}           & 320 & $-5.4\,\%$ & $9.0\,\%$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\noindent As the steps shrink, every channel marches \emph{downward} (Figure~\ref{fig:refine}). The fix's
near-perfect mean is partly a \textbf{cancellation}: backward-Euler biases $\dbdt$ high, while the spatial
discretization on this mesh biases it low ($\sim$3--6\,\%, the same floor seen on the circle in
Figure~\ref{fig:conv}). At $\times1.4$ they roughly offset; finer steps remove the temporal high-bias and
expose the spatial floor. This is the practical lesson on time-stepping: \textbf{be conservative, not
extreme}. The ramp must be gentle enough and have enough steps to resolve the early-time decay, but driving
$\Delta t$ arbitrarily small only trades one bias for another.

\begin{figure}[H]\centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.86\textwidth]{fig_timestep_refinement.png}
  \caption{\textbf{Why ``more time-steps'' is not enough.} Per-channel error versus the 1D layered square
  loop as the ramp is refined. The temporal high-bias (red) gives way to a spatial floor several percent
  low; the $\times1.4$ fix (blue) threads near zero.}
  \label{fig:refine}
\end{figure}

\noindent Driving \emph{every} channel below 1\,\% independently (not by cancellation) is a mesh-refinement
exercise --- targeted near-surface/source resolution to remove the spatial floor --- combined with the
conservative time-stepping above. The mean is already within $\sim$0.5\,\%, and against the same-geometry
1D square reference (Figure~\ref{fig:hero}) the headline result is within $\sim$3\,\% at every channel.

takes $\sim$11\,min and every later rebuild loads from disk.
